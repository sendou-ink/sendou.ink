sendou.ink draws the overlays of a tournament cast for you: rosters, map list, the game's scoreboard, results, the bracket and the hold screens. You run the stream from the **Broadcast** tab of the tournament, OBS shows whatever is on air. No other software is needed.

The tab is there for the tournament's organizers and streamers.

There is one broadcast per cast channel. A match marked as casted on a channel (from the match page) shows up on that channel's overlays by itself. Its scores, teams, rosters, maps and the bracket all come from the tournament automatically. The game and break scenes also scroll through the scores of the tournament's other sets, the ones no channel is casting: those being played and, for 10 minutes after their last game, those just finished. Towards the end of the tournament, with fewer than 5 of those, the most recently finished sets fill in.

:::tip[Theming is just CSS]
Most of making the overlays your own is plain CSS, the language every website is styled with. It's something anyone can learn, these are good places to start:

- web.dev's [Learn CSS](https://web.dev/learn/css), a course from the basics up
- MDN's [Styling the content](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Getting_started/Your_first_website/Styling_the_content), a short first look
:::

## Setting up OBS

You need OBS 31 or newer. An older OBS can't run the overlay: its browser source shows a card asking you to update OBS instead.

<Steps>

1. Open the tournament's Broadcast tab and pick the channel. This is the dashboard.
2. In the dashboard's **Setup** tab, press **Generate URL**. The URL is yours, the same for every channel and tournament, and has a secret key, so only you can show the overlay. It keeps working until you generate a new one, so you set up OBS once and leave it.
3. In OBS, create a new scene collection (Scene Collection → New) so your own setup stays untouched.
4. Set the canvas to 1920×1080 (Settings → Video).
5. Add your capture card, and on top of it a **Browser** source with the URL, sized 1920×1080. Check **Control audio via OBS** in its properties, so the casters and the music reach the stream.
6. So the game isn't heard under the other scenes: in OBS, open Tools → WebSocket Server Settings, check **Enable WebSocket server** and press **Show Connect Info** for its port and password. Fill them in under **Capture card audio** in the dashboard's **Setup** tab (they're saved with your URL, so once is enough), then pick the source the game's sound comes from (the capture card, or its Audio Input Capture if the sound has its own source). The list comes from OBS when the dashboard is open on the computer OBS runs on; elsewhere, type the source's name exactly as in OBS's Sources list.

</Steps>

That's all OBS needs: the browser source switches scenes by itself, and mutes the capture card on every scene but Game, unmuting it once the game is back on. It only touches the source you named, the rest of your audio stays as it is. Switch scenes and watch the capture card in OBS's audio mixer to check it works: with a stinger it changes once the stinger covers the screen. Leave the name empty to have the browser source not touch the capture card's audio, which unmutes it. On the dashboard, **Next** is what you have staged and **Take** puts it on air (Live) with the transition you picked. Queue scenes to go on air one after another, on a timer or as games start and end.

The stinger picks what it shows by itself: the match's teams facing off when going into or out of a game, and the tournament's logo and name between other scenes.

Your URL shows one broadcast at a time, the one where you last pressed **Show this broadcast**. For the next tournament or another channel, open that channel's dashboard and press **Show this broadcast** in the **Setup** tab: the OBS sources using your URL switch over right away, nothing to change in OBS. They stop showing the broadcast they showed before, so the dashboard asks first when your URL shows another one. Between tournaments it keeps showing the last broadcast. A channel's new broadcast starts with the theme and show info of the channel's last broadcast in the same organization.

**Generate new URL** makes the old URL stop working and the OBS sources using it go blank. Paste the new one into OBS afterwards.

If the browser source can't load completely, e.g. while sendou.ink restarts for an update, it shows nothing rather than a broken overlay and loads again by itself once sendou.ink answers. Once loaded, it keeps the last scene on air through any outage and catches up when the connection is back.

### When sendou.ink restarts or the connection drops

sendou.ink restarts for every update, and Wi-Fi can drop. Your show keeps its current scene meanwhile: the overlay in OBS stays as it is and catches up with whatever you did as soon as it is connected again, without you touching OBS. The dashboard shows a banner over the page while it is disconnected ("sendou.ink is restarting for an update" during an update, which takes a few seconds), dims the OBS and scanner pills as they may be out of date, and shows next to **Live** when it last heard from sendou.ink. **Take** and the other buttons changing what is on air are off until it is back, as the dashboard can't show what they did. Anything else you press that doesn't reach sendou.ink says "Not done" and changes nothing, so press it again once it's back.

If sendou.ink was updated in a way the open dashboard can't show, it says so: refresh the page to keep running the show from it.

## Casters' cameras

Add the casters in the dashboard's **Setup** tab, each with their VDO.ninja view link (`https://vdo.ninja/?view=…`):

- The Casters scene shows their camera. With **No camera** on it shows their avatar, ringed while they speak. Without a link, just the avatar.
- They're heard on every scene, so don't also add their audio to OBS.
- Next to their names the Casters scene and the game scene's info card show their pronouns and their sendou.ink user page (e.g. `/u/sendou`, if they have set a custom URL on their profile), taking turns when they have both.
- The **Mixer** under the live preview has a fader for each caster that applies right away, next to a meter of how loud they are on air at that volume, so you can match the casters' levels. If a caster's VDO.ninja connection drops, their strip turns red and a warning shows at the top of the dashboard. It shows **Not in OBS** while no OBS source has the overlay open.
- Check **Control audio via OBS** on the browser source, or they won't reach the stream.

The dashboard's previews and the designer show placeholders and play no sound. Anyone with a view link can watch it, so add `&password=` in VDO.ninja if needed.

## Music

The hold scenes (starting soon, break, standby and ending) and the casters scene show what's playing on a **now playing** line behind a note icon: the title and artist of a track of your music, or your own text. On the hold scenes it sits under the title (or the headline), on the casters scene low in the left corner.

- Drop your music files into the dashboard's **Music** tab. They stay in that browser only and go straight from it to your OBS sources, never through sendou.ink. Only add music you have the rights to play on stream.
- Each track's title and artist come from the file. Edit them there if needed.
- The tracks play in the library's order, looping, or with **Shuffle** on in a new mixed order each time through (with three or more tracks, never the same track twice in a row). **Play**, **Pause**, skip, shuffle and the volume are under the live preview and apply right away.
- The music is heard only on those scenes. It fades out as you Take another scene and back in as you return, playing on in the meantime. An OBS source that reloads picks up where the music is, and keeps the files it was sent, so they aren't sent again.
- Check **Control audio via OBS** on the browser source, or the music won't reach the stream.
- Playing music on your own computer instead? Type what's playing under **Now playing** below the live preview, next to the flavour text. It shows on the same line while no music plays from the dashboard, and the field is there while no music is in the dashboard's library.

The dashboard's previews and the designer show the line but play no sound.

## The scanner

The scanner watches the game feed and tells the broadcast when games start and end, so scenes set to wait for a game start or end go on air by themselves, in the teams' ink colors.

<Steps>

1. Open sendou.ink, logged in as you, in the browser that sees the game feed without the overlay over it: your capture card, or OBS Virtual Camera of a scene with only the capture card.
2. Start the scanner from the page's header. The first time, start it on the [scanner page](https://sendou.ink/scanner) instead, where you pick the capture source.

</Steps>

There is nothing to link: the scanner follows the broadcast your browser source URL shows, and switches over with it when you press **Show this broadcast**. It does so from a day before the tournament starts until the tournament is finalized, so scanning your own games between tournaments leaves the last broadcast alone. The dashboard shows "Scanner connected" once it's there.

Games still upload to sendou.ink as usual.

Alpha is the team on the left of the overlays, the same team the game's HUD shows on the left. Set it with **Swap** between the two team names under the live preview: it applies to every scene right away, on air and staged alike, so you can fix it mid-game without a Take. Each game's ink colors only need the pair: the scanner reads it, or pick it in the dashboard if the scanner isn't connected or misses it. The pair's first color always goes to Alpha.

## Replays

While it follows a broadcast, the scanner cuts clips of the cast's big moments: double and triple splats, splat streaks, longer runs of splats across camera switches, leads taken and big objective pushes. Moments close together become one longer clip. Pick one in the Replays scene's **Clip** list, newest first, and it plays on air for as long as it lasts.

The clips stay in the browser running the scanner, so only the broadcast's dashboard open there lists them. It sends them straight to OBS, which needs the scanner and OBS on the same computer (or network): the picked ones first, then the newest few ahead of time, so a clip is usually there before you pick it. Until OBS has a clip, the queue warns about it and on air the scene shows the footage OBS has under the overlay.

## The designer

The dashboard's **Theme** tab opens the designer: the overlay over a gameplay screenshot on the left, its theme on the right. Pick a scene and a situation (short or long names, how far the set is, ink colors, missing logos, …) to see your theme where it is most likely to break, and **Play transition** to watch the stinger, VS or tournament.

The right side reads top to bottom:

- **Preset**: ready-made themes (Zine, Matchday, Capsule, Teletext) to make your own from. Picking one fills the settings with its colors, fonts and sizes and puts the rest of its CSS in Custom CSS. Their CSS is also the best example of how far a theme can go, see [the presets' source](https://github.com/sendou-ink/sendou.ink/tree/main/app/features/broadcast-cast/presets).
- **Every scene**: the weapon images and the theme's colors, background image, fonts, corners and animation timing, no CSS needed. An empty setting shows its default; the button next to a set one resets it.
- **This scene**: the sizes and spots of the scene previewed and the parts on it (e.g. the scoreboard's bar height, where the info card sits), in pixels and seconds. Pick another scene to get its own.
- **Advanced: custom CSS**: your own CSS for everything else, at most {cssLimit} characters. "Default CSS of the … scene" shows the default styles of the scene previewed, read-only, to copy rules from.

Nothing changes on air before you **Save**, then every OBS source restyles right away and you're back on the dashboard. The theme and the weapon images are only set in the designer. The **Theme** tab shows which theme is on: the default styles, a preset or your own.

## How theme CSS works

The overlay is a 1920×1080 canvas, `[data-broadcast]`. You style it with four things:

- **Variables**: colors, fonts, corners, sizes, spots and animation timing, e.g. `--bc-accent` or `--game-bar-height`. Most themes are mostly this, and the designer's settings set the most used ones for you. In Custom CSS, set them on `[data-broadcast]`.
- **Scenes and parts**: `[data-scene="game"]` is on each scene, `[data-part="team-name"]` on the things in it. `[data-part="team-name"]` alone styles the team names of every scene.
- **State attributes**, next to the parts: `data-team` (`a` left, `b` right), `data-winner`, `data-loser`, `data-status` (e.g. a map's `played`, `current`, `upcoming`, `skipped`), `data-result` (`win`, `loss`), `data-featured`, `data-tbd`, and the stinger's `data-variant` (`teams`, `tournament`).
- **Keyframes**: the animations, `bc-enter`, `bc-reveal`, … Redefine one to change everything using it, or point a `--bc-anim-*` variable at keyframes of your own.

Custom CSS beats the default styles without `!important` or specificity tricks. The settings are saved as one more `[data-broadcast] { … }` block after it, so a setting you set wins over the same variable set on `[data-broadcast]` in Custom CSS (one set on a part or scene still applies there). The default CSS the designer shows refers to the default styles' classes as `.Stylesheet-class`, e.g. `.GameScene-infoCard`; they work in Custom CSS too, for the few elements that have no part.

## Fonts

The overlay uses two fonts: `--bc-font-body` for everything, and `--bc-font-display` for titles, names and scores, which is the body font unless you set it. The presets' fonts come with the overlay, use them by name without loading anything: `Outfit`, `"Barlow Condensed"`, `"Space Grotesk"`, `"Archivo Black"` and `VT323`. Any font on the web works too, [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/) has plenty free to use:

<Steps>

1. Find a font on [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/) and open it, e.g. Barlow Condensed.
2. Press **Get font**, then **Get embed code**.
3. Pick the weights you want (the overlay uses semibold, bold and heavy: 600, 700 and 800), then choose **@import** under Web.
4. Copy only the `@import url(...);` line, without the `<style>` tags around it, to the very top of Custom CSS.
5. Set the font's name, as shown under "CSS class" on the same page, as the overlay's font (titles, names and scores follow it):

</Steps>

```css
@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Barlow+Condensed:wght@600;700;800&display=swap");

[data-broadcast] {
	--bc-font-body: "Barlow Condensed", sans-serif;
}
```

A font with fewer weights than the overlay uses: set `--bc-weight-semibold`, `--bc-weight-bold` and `--bc-weight-heavy` to the ones it has. A font from elsewhere: point an `@font-face` at its `.woff2` file, the site hosting it has to allow other sites to load it.

```css
@font-face { font-family: "My Font"; src: url("https://example.com/my-font.woff2") format("woff2"); }
[data-broadcast] { --bc-font-display: "My Font", sans-serif; }
```

OBS loads the font from the internet each time the browser source starts, just after the overlay is up (so a slow font site never holds up the overlay), and the text shows in the fallback font until then. Keep a fallback (`sans-serif`) after its name.

## With this CSS → this effect

```css
[data-broadcast] { --bc-accent: #ffd400; }
```
Every highlight in yellow.

```css
[data-broadcast] { --bc-team-a: #ff5f1f; --bc-team-b: #1fa2ff; }
```
Team colors orange and blue (the game and result scenes use the game's ink colors once they're known).

```css
[data-broadcast] { --bc-radius: 0; --bc-radius-small: 0; }
```
Square corners everywhere.

```css
@import url("https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Barlow+Condensed:wght@600;700&display=swap");
[data-broadcast] { --bc-font-display: "Barlow Condensed", sans-serif; }
```
Titles, names and scores in Barlow Condensed.

```css
[data-part="team-name"] { text-transform: uppercase; }
```
Team names in capitals, in every scene.

```css
[data-scene="game"] [data-part="roster"] { display: none; }
```
Game scene without the player names above the nameplates.

```css
[data-part="caster-pronouns"] { display: none; }
[data-part="caster-url"] { animation: none; }
```
No pronouns next to the casters' names, only their user page (without `animation: none` it would still fade out half the time).

```css
[data-broadcast] { --bc-caster-details-interval: 10s; }
```
Casters' pronouns and user pages take turns every 10 seconds.

```css
[data-scene="break"] [data-part="ticker"] { display: none; }
```
Break without the other matches' scores.

```css
[data-part="now-playing"][data-source="text"] { display: none; }
```
The now playing card only for music, not for your text.

```css
[data-winner] [data-part="team-name"] { color: var(--bc-accent); }
```
The winning team's name in the accent color.

```css
[data-scene="game"] [data-part="scoreboard"] { bottom: 40px; }
```
Game scoreboard 40px up from the bottom edge.

```css
[data-broadcast] { --game-bar-height: 84px; --game-team-name-size: 40px; }
```
Taller scoreboard, bigger team names.

```css
.GameScene-corner { left: auto; right: 28px; align-items: flex-end; }
```
Game scene's info card and map intro in the top right.

```css
[data-broadcast] { --game-map-intro-duration: 4s; }
```
Map intro leaves after 4 seconds.

```css
[data-part="backdrop"] { background: #000 url("https://example.com/bg.png") center / cover; }
```
Your own image behind the full screen scenes.

```css
[data-broadcast] { --bc-enter-duration: 900ms; --bc-stagger: 120ms; }
```
Things come in slower, further apart.

```css
[data-broadcast] { --bc-anim-enter: none; }
```
No entrance animations, everything is just there.

```css
@keyframes bc-enter { from { opacity: 0; scale: 0.9; } }
```
Everything grows in instead of sliding in.

```css
[data-part="team-score"] { --bc-anim-count: flip; }
@keyframes flip { from { transform: rotateX(90deg); } }
```
Scores flip in when they change.

```css
[data-part="stinger-card"] { display: none; }
```
Stinger without the card in its middle (the teams, or the tournament's logo and name).

```css
[data-part="stinger"][data-variant="tournament"] [data-part="stinger-tournament-logo"] { border-radius: 0; }
```
The tournament stinger's logo square instead of round.

```css
[data-part="stinger-title-backdrop"] { display: none; }
```
Tournament stinger without the tournament and organization names drifting in the background.

## Good to know

- `@import`s (web fonts) have to come first in Custom CSS, or the browser ignores them.
- The stinger changes the scene at `--bc-stinger-duration` × `--bc-stinger-covered-at` (704 ms by default). Cuts to and from a game are timed for that moment, so keep it close to 704 ms if you retime the stinger.
- Keyframes that pop in with `steps(1, end)` can get stuck hidden: an animation can end a hair short in Chrome. Use `steps(n, jump-none)`, or plain keyframes holding the hidden state until 99%.
- To retime an animation, set `animation-duration` or `animation-delay` rather than the whole `animation`: its keyframes, easing and fill stay as they are. With two animations on a part (in and out), give two comma separated values.
- Everything is in pixels on the 1920×1080 canvas, no need for responsive CSS.
- The class names you see in the browser's dev tools (e.g. `_infoCard_1x2y3`) change with every update. Use parts, variables and `.Stylesheet-class` names instead.

## What stays stable

Themes are saved as CSS, so renaming anything they refer to breaks them. These names are the theme's contract and are only renamed when there is no way around it:

- `data-scene` values, `data-part` names and the state attributes
- the variables (`--bc-*` and each stylesheet's own, e.g. `--game-*`)
- the `bc-*` keyframes
- the stylesheet and class names (`.GameScene-infoCard`)

The markup around the parts (which element is inside which, their order) can change, so select by parts rather than by structure.
