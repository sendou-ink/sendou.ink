#### *Eleven years of AREA CUP. Four brand-new champions. One Western player.*

<img width="1200" height="675" alt="firsttimeAREACUPwin" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f9db74bc-9fab-4983-8db8-7e730ffea3b3" />

On July 11th, 2026, sixteen teams went head-to-head in the 61st edition of Japan’s most prestigious community-run tournament series, AREA CUP. Despite being an international tournament, AREA CUP has maintained a 100% Japanese win rate since its inception in 2015\.

That is, until AREA CUP 61 came around and marked a new milestone for the global Competitive Splatoon scene.

AREA CUP is a Splat Zones-only tournament that runs about twice per month. The tournament has a Groups to Single-Elimination format—no room for second chances; one set loss, and it’s game over. The competition is primarily made up of the best players in Japan, many who have at least one AREA CUP win decorating their competitive record.

It was in this event that Full house, a team made up of Renorua, Razzy, Syomi, and Grey, wrote a new chapter in AREA CUP’s history books.

If you only keep up with Competitive Splatoon in the West, the first three names may not be familiar to you, but rest assured, you know the fourth one: Grey, of PxG and Alliance Rogue fame, Blaster-class main, with a 100% first-place win rate in 2026 in the Western scene up to this point.

The European player has won at least one each of various Western tournament majors, such as SuperJump, 20XX, and SendouQ Season Finales; LANs including Europe’s Fullwipe and the United States’ Riptide; and is a Nintendo European champion.

Splatoon is taken to a higher level in Japan compared to the West, with a larger and more varied competitive scene, unified timezones, and closer proximity. Top Western players are known to take part in Japanese tournaments from time to time to further hone their skill.

This is where the story of AREA CUP 61 begins.

## **The Team**

Per Grey’s own words, there “wasn’t really a plan” for him to participate in AREA CUP 61\. The day before the tournament, Renorua, a JP Dynamo Roller player, contacted Grey and asked if he wanted to play on a team with himself, Razzy (.96 Gal), and Syomi (Tri-Stringer), who were all Japanese players. 

Together, the four players registered under the team name “Full house”. No one on the team had won an AREA CUP tournament before. Their combined team X Power average was about 3400 XP.

*Full house’s collective X Power average, 3395 XP. From left to right: Grey, Razzy, Syomi, Renorua. Source: Grey’s July 16, 2026 YouTube video, “I Entered the Hardest Splatoon Tournament…”*

Most of their opponents would be AREA CUP winners—some having won more than once—with a team average between 3700 and 3800 XP. In the Top 8, every one of Full house’s opponents had one player above 4000 XP.

In his tournament video on YouTube, “I Entered the Hardest Splatoon Tournament…”, Grey mentioned that while he isn’t a fluent Japanese speaker, he still did his best to communicate his callouts in Japanese.

## **The Tournament**

Full house made it past the Groups stage and into the Top 8, where the Quarterfinals were a Best of Three, and the Semifinals and Finals were a Best of Five. All of Full house’s sets in the Top 8 went to the furthest possible game. 

Full house won their Quarterfinals set 2–1 against かっしーおぶはむ (translated name: Kasshi of Ham), which featured two players who would later appear in the Splat World Series 2026 Playoffs, Tanaha and Kasiwa. 

The Semifinals were Full house’s greatest comeback. They began the set down 0–2 against サーマルようちゃん (translated name: Thermal Yo-chan) and needed to reverse-sweep to advance. During game five at Crableg Capital, Full house clutched the win in overtime, making it to the Finals.

And then Full house found themselves in the championship match, versus れたえるっとたん, whose team name, when translated, is Reta-eru-tto-tan, made from one part of each of the team’s players’ names. 

The teams went toe-to-toe with victories; neither was ahead by more than one game at any point. 

“So it was 1-1 at this point and they counterpicked \[Bluefin Depot\] into us. Honestly, I thought it was cooked. But, you know, never go into a game thinking that you’re going to lose.” —Grey, about game 3 of the Finals set

First, れたえるっとたん won at Barnacle & Dime. Full house won the next two games, at Urchin Underpass and Bluefin Depot. れたえるっとたん tied the set 2–2 after taking the victory at Robo ROM-en. Game five came down to Flounder Heights, which Full house won by a knockout\! 

## **The Success Factors**

How does one win an AREA CUP? Why has it taken so long for a Western player to win one? The West has won tournaments that included Japanese teams before; it’s not an impossible feat.

Grey’s video about his AREA CUP 61 run is primarily a tournament showcase, with a few asides added for context. On the same day that his “I Entered the Hardest Splatoon Tournament…” video went up, Splatoon World Champion and coach Hexen also posted a video on his YouTube channel, titled “This Has Never Been Done Before”, where he and Grey analyzed some of Full house’s games and discussed how such an underdog team came out of Japan’s premier community tournament as champions.

In a tournament with competition as stacked as AREA CUP, it’s just not enough to have a team full of strong players—everyone must be individually capable as much as they have to be able to work effectively together. Nor is it enough to only be running the best weapons in the current meta; the opposing team compositions and the maps must also be heavily considered. 

<img width="1000" height="562" alt="howdouapproachthis" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/6abf2dab-4baa-4d0c-b02d-bfdb7eba7b1e" />

*“Also, your comp, you have Sensors, CBlaster, and Dynamo; how do the enemies approach you at all? It’s extremely hard.” —Hexen, on Full house’s weapon choices.*

Highlighted in Hexen’s video was Full house’s weapon synergy. With the Dynamo Roller and Tri-Stringer capable of quickly putting down wide areas of ink paired with Custom Blaster’s Point Sensor, it was very difficult for enemies to get close. Especially on a map like Urchin Underpass, with its many levels of height difference, small zone, and tight spaces.

Another highlight from the video is individual player mechanics and map awareness. Renorua is not one of Japan’s best Dynamo Rollers just because the Dynamo Roller excels at painting and splatting. He excels at moving with the heavyweight weapon by being opportunistic with the tiniest ink patches, and using his Sprinkler sub-weapon to escape unfavorable positions when he identifies that there’s not enough time to swing.

Hexen’s video, “[This Has Never Been Done Before](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QY9WRtNsP94)” on YouTube, covers one game from each player’s perspective, and is a great watch for anyone who wants to see a VOD review of JP gameplay with an analysis in English. 

Grey’s YouTube video, “[I Entered the Hardest Splatoon Tournament…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OvTnCNtclOI)”, is his AREA CUP 61 run with Renorua, Razzy, and Syomi, starting at the Quarterfinals. Every game that they played in the Top 8 is in the video, and the description includes a resource for JP callouts and a link to the bracket spreadsheet.

There is also the [AREA CUP 61 stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EwpwfMHSAXs) to check out, which also begins at the Quarterfinals, and includes the post-tournament interview with Full house. The stream is in Japanese, but during the interview portion, Hemi does translate questions into English for Grey.

AREA CUP, for the first time in its 10+ years, has finally reached the end of an era. The historic win, which saw an entire team achieve their first-ever AREA CUP victory, also shattered the tournament’s 100% JP-team win rate when one of those players also earned the first-ever AREA CUP win from the West.

The hope is that this milestone serves as inspiration for more Western players to compete in JP tournaments. The climb to the highest peak is not an easy one, but not an impossible one, either\!
